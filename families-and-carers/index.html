Families and carers guide

If you're a partner, parent, family member or friend supporting someone with BPD (or who may have BPD), there are free peer groups, courses and national carer services you can use. This page lists what each official site publishes about who it's for, how to join, cost and location. It doesn't recommend one option over another.

In danger or need to talk now?

If you or someone else is in immediate danger, call 000.

  • 000 Emergency (police, ambulance, fire)
  • 13 11 14 Lifeline (24/7)
  • 1300 659 467 Suicide Call Back Service (24/7)
  • 1300 22 4636 Beyond Blue
  • 13 92 76 13YARN (Aboriginal & Torres Strait Islander crisis support)
  • 1300 555 788 WA Mental Health Emergency Response Line: Perth metro

More numbers, including Peel, country WA and alcohol & drugs

Not medical advice. This is a directory of publicly listed carer supports. It doesn't diagnose, treat, or tell you what will work for your situation. Always check details with the service before you join.

The short version

  • In WA, MIFWA runs a free monthly BPD Carer Support Group (Zoom and West Leederville) plus other carer coffee and Grow groups.
  • Family Connections is a free 12-week course for adult carers of someone with BPD, run across Australia by NEABPD-Australia (waitlist).
  • Project Air (University of Wollongong) has a free online carer e-learning program and fact sheets.
  • Carer Gateway is the Australian Government's free service for unpaid carers (peer support, counselling, coaching, respite pathways).
  • Victoria's Spectrum and South Australia's BPD Collaborative also publish free BPD carer workshops and groups (mainly for people in those states).
  • WA carer helplines listed by the Mental Health Commission include CarersWA, Carer Gateway and Helping Minds.

MIFWA (Mental Illness Fellowship of WA)

MIFWA's carer pages describe supports for carers, families and supporters of people with mental health challenges, including a BPD-specific group.

  • BPD Carer Support Group: free monthly peer-facilitated group for family, friends and carers supporting someone with BPD or similar traits. Runs 5.30pm–7.00pm on the second Wednesday of the month, alternating between in person in West Leederville and online via Zoom. Registration is essential (phone call with the Carer Support team). Contact (08) 9237 8900 or carersupport@mifwa.org.au. MIFWA says the group is not suitable for workers or for people with a lived experience of BPD unless they are also in an active caring role.
  • Other carer groups: coffee clubs across Perth metro, and Grow Groups for carers in Rockingham/Waikiki and Midland (no fee; Grow Groups say no RSVP and no referral needed).
  • One-to-one carer support and family education (including a Building a Future program) are also listed on MIFWA's carers page; details of cost and wait for those are not published on the pages checked below beyond the group listings.

Spectrum (Victoria)

Spectrum is Victoria's statewide personality disorder service (Eastern Health). It publishes education and support for families, friends, supporters and kin of someone with (or a suspected diagnosis of) BPD. Workshops are co-facilitated by a lived-experience carer and a clinician.

  • One-day carer workshop: in person at Spectrum's Richmond office, for unpaid/voluntary carers. Spectrum says it is not suited to people with lived experience of BPD unless they are actively caring for someone else with BPD, and is not intended for clinicians, mental health workers or paid carers.
  • Short online carer workshop: a 3-hour online version of the one-day workshop.
  • Carer Support Group: online, first Wednesday of each month (excluding January), 6:30pm–8:00pm AEST. Victoria residents only.
  • Cost: not published on the Spectrum pages checked. Contact: (03) 8413 8750, spectrumcarers@easternhealth.org.au.

BPD Community (Victoria)

Carers Victoria still publishes a listing for a BPD Community Family & Friends Group: peer-led Zoom support on the third Tuesday of every month, 6.30pm–8.30pm, with BPD-informed psychoeducation. Cost: no. Contact listed as Barb Mullen, 0409 952 754, bpdcommunityvic@gmail.com. The listing links to bpdcommunity.com.au for more information.

Important check (5 Nov 2026): the organisation's own website at bpdcommunity.com.au was unavailable when we tried to open it. An earlier public notice on that site said BPD Community Ltd was closing operations as of 30 June 2025 and pointed people to Aviiara Health Psychology for ongoing clinical support. Whether the Family & Friends Group is still running is not published on a working official page we could load. Confirm with the Carers Victoria contact details above, or Carers Victoria on 1800 514 845, before planning to attend.

SA Health BPD Collaborative (South Australia)

SA Health's BPD Collaborative publishes a free carer peer program for unpaid carers, families, partners and friends of someone living with BPD. Workshops are open to people supporting someone living in SA.

  • Carer information sessions (2 hours): monthly online "Introduction to BPD" sessions covering understanding BPD, communication skills, self-care and links to supports.
  • Carer peer group (4 weeks): "Supporting Someone Recently Diagnosed with BPD" — online or face-to-face for unpaid supporters of people recently diagnosed or likely to have BPD. Topics listed: symptoms/treatments/recovery, carer role, the SA mental health system, self-care and a carer plan.
  • Family Connections (12 weeks): coordinated by NEABPD-Australia; metro Adelaide groups plus online for regional carers. Register on the NEABPD waitlist.
  • Cost: all workshops listed as free. Contact: (08) 7425 6500, health.BPDservice@sa.gov.au. Future dates via their Eventbrite collection (linked from the SA Health page).

Source: SA Health: Carers, families, partners and friends supporting people with BPD · Last checked 5 Nov 2026

Family Connections (NEABPD-Australia)

Family Connections is a free 12-week course that meets weekly for two hours. BPD Australia (NEABPD-Australia) says it provides education, skills training and support for people in a relationship with someone who has BPD. Groups are hosted in community settings and led by trained group leaders who are clinicians or family members. Content listed includes education on BPD, relationship mindfulness, emotion regulation, communication, validation and problem management skills.

  • Who: family members and carers of individuals with BPD. Not suitable for people under 18, or for loved ones with BPD / BPD traits / emotion dysregulation (the person with BPD does not attend as a participant).
  • Access: register interest for the waitlist first; when a group has spots, email the address published on the classes page. Places are filled in registration-reply order. Groups are listed as available across Australia; wait times are not published beyond a note that there has been a considerable waitlist.
  • Cost: free.

Project Air (University of Wollongong)

Project Air Strategy publishes free resources for families, partners and carers supporting someone with a personality disorder. It states clearly that it does not provide crisis services, support, counselling, advice, treatment or referrals — those come from health services.

  • Project Air Carers e-learning: free, available 24/7. Six modules (about 30–45 minutes each) covering the supportive role and wellbeing, personality disorder information, a carer plan, relationship patterns, communication, and crisis situations. A certificate of completion is provided.
  • Fact sheets and videos for families, partners and carers are linked from the same carer page.

Source: University of Wollongong — Project Air: I am a parent, carer or guardian · Last checked 5 Nov 2026

Carer Gateway (Australian Government)

Carer Gateway is a free Australian Government program for unpaid carers of a family member or friend with disability, a medical condition, mental illness, or who is frail due to age. Services listed include in-person and online peer support groups, tailored support packages (including planned respite and transport), counselling, self-guided coaching, online skills courses, and emergency respite.

  • Phone: 1800 422 737, Monday to Friday, 8am–5pm (select option 1 to speak with your local service provider about registration and planning).
  • A separate Carer Gateway page on caring for someone with mental illness also lists the Mental Health Carer Helpline on 1300 554 660, and points to state crisis lines (for WA: MHERL metro 1300 555 788 / country 1800 676 822 — note country number on that page; WA MHC also publishes Rurallink 1800 552 002 for after-hours country support).
  • Carer Gateway is for carers generally — it is not BPD-specific.

Source: Carer Gateway: What is Carer Gateway? · Last checked 5 Nov 2026

Other WA carer contacts (Mental Health Commission)

The WA Mental Health Commission's helplines page lists these carer support lines (among others):

  • CarersWA general line: 1300 227 377 (Mon–Fri 8.30am–4.30pm); counselling line 1800 007 332 (same hours).
  • Carer Gateway: 1800 422 737.
  • Helping Minds: 1800 811 747 (also (08) 9427 7100). Helping Minds publishes free confidential support for mental health carers living in WA (aged 7+), including counselling, peer support, education and Carer Gateway pathways. It says a formal diagnosis is not always required, and it is not a crisis service.

Questions you can ask a carer service

  • "Who is this group or course for, and who shouldn't join?"
  • "Is it free? Are there any other costs?"
  • "Do I need a referral, or do I just register?"
  • "Is it in person, online, or both? Which areas / states?"
  • "How long is the wait, and how do I join a cancellation list if you have one?"
  • "Is it peer-led, clinician-led, or both?"
