Private hospital DBT and health insurance: a checklist

Some private hospitals in Perth run DBT day programs and inpatient care. Most people pay for these through private health insurance. This page explains, in plain words, how hospital cover works for psychiatric care, the waiting periods, and what to ask your fund and the hospital before you start. It doesn't recommend any fund, policy or hospital.

The short version

  • Private hospital psychiatric care is paid for under hospital cover, not extras.
  • Every hospital policy (Basic, Bronze, Silver, Gold) has to include hospital psychiatric services, but only Gold must cover them fully. Basic, Bronze and Silver policies are allowed to cover them on a restricted basis, which can leave big out-of-pocket costs in a private hospital.
  • The longest waiting period a fund can set for hospital psychiatric care is 2 months, even if the condition is pre-existing.
  • If you're on restricted psychiatric cover and have held hospital cover for 2 months, you can upgrade to higher psychiatric cover without another wait, once in your lifetime.
  • Perth's private programs listed on this site all ask for a GP or specialist referral, and most need you to be under, or assessed by, one of their psychiatrists.

Restricted vs full cover for psychiatric care

Since April 2020 every hospital policy in Australia is sorted into one of four tiers: Gold, Silver, Bronze or Basic. The government's table of tiers says:

TierHospital psychiatric services
BasicMust be included, but can be restricted
BronzeMust be included, but can be restricted
SilverMust be included, but can be restricted
GoldMust be included, unrestricted

A restricted benefit, in the government's words, means you're partly covered for hospital costs as a private patient in a public hospital, and "you may incur significant expenses in a private room or private hospital". Some funds sell "Plus" policies (like Bronze Plus or Silver Plus) that cover more than the minimum for their tier, so a Plus policy might include unrestricted psychiatric cover. The only way to know is to check the policy itself.

Source: privatehealth.gov.au (Commonwealth Ombudsman): Product tiers · Last checked 4 Nov 2026

Waiting periods

The government sets the most a fund can make you wait before claiming hospital benefits after you join or upgrade:

  • 2 months for psychiatric care, rehabilitation and palliative care, even for a pre-existing condition.
  • 12 months for other pre-existing conditions and for pregnancy and birth.
  • 2 months for everything else.

Waiting periods for extras (like psychology on your extras cover) are set by each fund, not the government.

Source: privatehealth.gov.au (Commonwealth Ombudsman): Waiting periods · Last checked 4 Nov 2026

The once-in-a-lifetime mental health upgrade

The Department of Health explains the rule like this:

  • If you've finished a 2-month waiting period on a policy with limited psychiatric benefits, you can upgrade to higher psychiatric cover without serving another waiting period.
  • You can do this at any time, but only once in your lifetime. Each person on a policy has their own once-only exemption.
  • It covers hospital (or hospital-substitute) care for mental health and for drug and alcohol treatment.
  • You can upgrade with your current fund or switch to any other fund, as long as you've held some hospital cover for 2 months with your current fund.
  • If you upgrade within 5 days of going into hospital, you may be covered for treatment from before the upgrade. After 5 days, cover starts from the upgrade date.
  • You only "use up" the exemption if you claim the higher benefits for mental health treatment within 2 months of upgrading.
  • Your old excess or co-payment may still apply until the normal 2 months has passed, and the new, higher premium starts from the day you upgrade. You can drop back to lower cover later.
  • If a hospital can't confirm your cover while you're upgrading, it may ask for an upfront fee; whether that's refunded is up to the hospital.

Source: Department of Health, Disability and Ageing: Waiting periods and exemptions · Last checked 4 Nov 2026

Checklist: questions to ask your health fund

  • "Is hospital psychiatric cover on my policy restricted or unrestricted?"
  • "Have I served the 2-month waiting period for psychiatric care? When did it end?"
  • "Have I already used my once-in-a-lifetime mental health upgrade exemption?"
  • "Does my policy cover day programs (like a weekly DBT group) as well as overnight stays?"
  • "Do you have an agreement with [hospital name]? What's my excess or co-payment there?"
  • "What will I pay out of pocket for the hospital, and for the psychiatrist's fees?"

Checklist: questions to ask the hospital

  • "Do I need a referral from my GP, or from a psychiatrist? Who is an admitting psychiatrist here?"
  • "Is there an intake assessment, and is there a charge for it?"
  • "How long is the wait for the next program?"
  • "Can you check my cover with my fund and tell me my out-of-pocket costs before I start?"
  • "What's the price if I pay for it myself (no insurance)?"

If you don't have private cover

Public mental health services and Medicare Mental Health Centres don't need private insurance. Our Costs and Medicare page covers what Medicare pays and the free options, and the services list includes public and community programs. The hospitals above also list DVA, WorkCover or paying for yourself as options where they publish it.

To compare policies, the government runs a free comparison site at privatehealth.gov.au. This guide doesn't compare or recommend funds and doesn't earn anything from them.
