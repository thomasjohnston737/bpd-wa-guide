While you wait

Waitlists for DBT programs, psychologists and psychiatrists can be long. This page lists free lines and services that official sources say you can use without a referral, carer supports, and what services publish about waits and earlier appointments. It doesn't tell you what to do or what will work for you.

In danger or need to talk now?

If you or someone else is in immediate danger, call 000.

  • 000 Emergency (police, ambulance, fire)
  • 13 11 14 Lifeline (24/7)
  • 1300 659 467 Suicide Call Back Service (24/7)
  • 1300 22 4636 Beyond Blue
  • 13 92 76 13YARN (Aboriginal & Torres Strait Islander crisis support)
  • 1300 555 788 WA Mental Health Emergency Response Line: Perth metro

More numbers, including Peel, country WA and alcohol & drugs

Not medical advice. This is a directory of free supports and published waitlist details. It doesn't diagnose, treat, or replace the service you're waiting for. If things get worse while you wait, the crisis lines above are open now.

The short version

  • Medicare Mental Health (formerly Head to Health) is a free phone line: 1800 595 212, weekdays 8:30am to 5pm (not public holidays). No GP referral, diagnosis or mental health treatment plan needed.
  • Medicare Mental Health Centres: WA has five (Armadale, Gosnells, Midland, Mirrabooka, Northam). Free and confidential for adults 18+, walk in or make an appointment. They are not a crisis service.
  • Here For You (WA, mental health and alcohol/other drugs): 1800 437 348, 7am to 10pm daily, with a WA-based counsellor.
  • Lifeline (13 11 14, 24/7), Beyond Blue (1300 22 4636, 24/7) and 13YARN (13 92 76, 24/7) are free national lines.
  • Carers can join MIFWA's free monthly BPD Carer Support Group, or register for the Family Connections waitlist.
  • Cancellation lists: none of the WA program pages we checked publish whether they keep one. You can ask the service when you're put on the waitlist.

Medicare Mental Health: phone line and centres

The Australian Government's Medicare Mental Health service says it is free, confidential and for everyone, and that you can get started without a GP referral, diagnosis or Mental Health Treatment Plan.

  • Phone: 1800 595 212. You speak with a trained professional and peer worker who can help you find support. Weekdays 8:30am to 5pm, excluding public holidays.
  • Online: the same site lists Medicare Mental Health Check In (digital tools, with telehealth if you need extra help) and Kids Hubs for families with children up to 12.

Source: Medicare Mental Health (Australian Government) · Last checked 10 Nov 2026

WA Primary Health Alliance (WAPHA) lists the five WA Medicare Mental Health Centres. Services are free and confidential for people aged 18 and over, and no referral or appointment is needed. Mental health professionals and people with lived experience work together at each centre. WAPHA says the centres will "soon" also offer free psychologist and psychiatrist access (in person or telehealth); a start date is not published.

Free phone and online lines

  • Here For You Support Line (WA Mental Health Commission): for anyone in WA seeking help with their own or someone else's mental health, alcohol and/or other drug challenges. 1800 437 348 (toll free), 7am to 10pm daily. One-to-one phone or online contact with a qualified counsellor based in WA, and guidance on local services. Not an emergency service. Email hereforyou@mhc.wa.gov.au or Live Chat for people with speech or hearing difficulties.
  • Lifeline: call 13 11 14, text 0477 13 11 14, or chat online. Lifeline says it provides 24-hour crisis support.
  • Beyond Blue: free phone counselling on 1300 22 4636 and webchat, both 24/7. Beyond Blue says it can also help you find mental health services if you need extra support.
  • 13YARN: 13 92 76, 24/7, with Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Crisis Supporters. Text 0447 13 92 76 or type online, 4pm to midnight, 7 days.
  • Other lines listed by the WA Mental Health Commission include Suicide Call Back Service (1300 659 467), MensLine Australia (1300 789 978), QLife (1800 184 527), Butterfly Foundation (1800 334 673), Healthdirect (1800 022 222) and, for 12 to 25-year-olds, headspace (1800 650 890, 3pm to 10pm) and Kids Helpline (1800 551 800).

If you're a family member or carer

  • MIFWA BPD Carer Support Group: monthly, 5:30pm to 7pm, alternating between in person in West Leederville and online via Zoom. Upcoming dates listed: 11 Nov 2026 and 9 Dec 2026 (online). Contact MIFWA on (08) 9237 8900, Monday to Friday 8.30am to 4.30pm. See our families and carers page for registration details and other groups.
  • Family Connections (NEABPD-Australia): free 12-week classes. You register your interest first to go on the waitlist, which is a requirement to join. When a group listed on their page has spots, you email them, and places are filled in the order replies arrive from people already registered. Not suitable for under-18s, or for the person with BPD. Wait times are not published.
  • Carer lines listed by the WA Mental Health Commission: CarersWA 1300 227 377 (general) and 1800 007 332 (counselling), both Mon to Fri 8.30am to 4.30pm; Carer Gateway 1800 422 737; Helping Minds 1800 811 747.

Waits, earlier appointments and cancellation lists

headspace's page on waiting for counselling says waiting periods vary with things like where you live and how many people are seeking support, and suggests checking with the service for a rough estimate. It also says that if things get harder while you wait, you can contact the service, which might be able to offer an earlier appointment, point you to other resources or give you support in the meantime.

Source: headspace: What to do while you wait for counselling · Last checked 10 Nov 2026

What the WA services on this site publish about waits:

  • Youth Axis and YouthLink (NMHS, ages 16 to 24 / 13 to 24) share one triage line: 1300 362 569. A current wait time is not published on the page.
  • Lifeline WA DBTeen (14 to 18 with a carer) runs by semester. Referrals for Semester 2 2026 are closed; referrals are open for Semester 1 2027, with groups starting from the second week of February 2027. You can only join at the start of a 12-week program.
  • Cancellation lists: whether a service keeps one is not published on any of the WA DBT and BPD program pages we checked today (NMHS, Perth Clinic, Ramsay Clinic Hollywood, Marian Centre, Abbotsford, NMHS Youth, Lifeline WA DBTeen). Ask the service directly.

Questions you can ask when you go on a waitlist

  • "Roughly how long is the wait right now?"
  • "Do you keep a cancellation list, and can I go on it?"
  • "How will you contact me, and what happens if I miss the call?"
  • "Do I need to check in to stay on the list?"
  • "Is there any support from you while I wait?"
  • "Who should I call if things get worse before my appointment?"
