Costs and Medicare: what you'll pay

How Medicare helps with the cost of seeing a psychologist or psychiatrist in Australia, what a "gap fee" is, how the Medicare Safety Net works, and which options are free. Figures come straight from official government pages, linked under each section.

In danger or need to talk now?

If you or someone else is in immediate danger, call 000.

  • 000 Emergency (police, ambulance, fire)
  • 13 11 14 Lifeline (24/7)
  • 1300 659 467 Suicide Call Back Service (24/7)
  • 1300 22 4636 Beyond Blue
  • 13 92 76 13YARN (Aboriginal & Torres Strait Islander crisis support)
  • 1300 555 788 WA Mental Health Emergency Response Line: Perth metro

More numbers, including Peel, country WA and alcohol & drugs

Not financial or medical advice. Fees change, usually each 1 July and 1 January. Always ask the clinic what you'll pay before you book.

The short version

  • With a mental health treatment plan and a referral, Medicare pays part of the cost of up to 10 individual and 10 group sessions each calendar year.
  • Health professionals set their own fees. If they charge more than Medicare pays, you pay the difference (the gap). If they bulk bill, you pay nothing.
  • Medicare Mental Health Centres and the Medicare Mental Health phone line are free, with no referral needed.
  • Public mental health services in hospitals and community clinics usually cost nothing, but there's usually a wait.

These are the official Medicare amounts for a standard individual session of at least 50 minutes, in person, with a referral. The schedule fee is the amount the government sets for the service; Medicare pays 85% of it. What the professional actually charges is up to them.

The rules that catch people out

  • The referral has to come from the right doctor. MBS Online says Medicare only pays if your referral comes from the GP practice where you're registered with MyMedicare, your usual GP (the practice that's given you most of your care over the past year, or will over the next year), or a psychiatrist or paediatrician.
  • The plan isn't the referral. A mental health treatment plan isn't the same thing as a referral; you need a written referral too.
  • Six at a time. The first referral covers up to 6 individual sessions. After that you need a review and a new referral for the rest of your 10.
  • Plans don't expire. You don't need a new plan every year, just a new referral when you've used the sessions you were referred for.
  • Medicare or your health fund, not both. You can't use private health insurance extras to top up the Medicare rebate on the same session. Once your Medicare sessions for the year are used up, you may be able to claim from your fund if you have psychology cover.
  • Not for public services. These Medicare items don't apply to services already funded by the state or Commonwealth, or if you're an admitted hospital patient.
  • Check how many you've used. You can see your care plan history in your Medicare online account through myGov, or call Medicare on 132 011.

The Medicare Safety Net

If your out-of-pocket costs for out-of-hospital services add up over the calendar year, you start getting more back once you pass a threshold. Gap fees for Medicare-eligible mental health sessions count towards it; costs for services Medicare doesn't cover don't.

2026 thresholdWho it's forAmount
Original Medicare Safety NetEveryone in Medicare$594.40 in gap amounts
Extended Medicare Safety Net: generalEveryone in Medicare$2,699.10 out of pocket
Extended Medicare Safety Net: concessionalConcession card holders and families getting Family Tax Benefit Part A$861.20 out of pocket

Once you pass the Extended threshold, Medicare pays back 80% of out-of-pocket costs, up to a cap for each item (for example, MBS Online lists a cap of $358.35 for item 80110). Only payments you've actually made count. Families and couples can register together to combine their costs.

Free options

  • Medicare Mental Health Centres: free for anyone in Australia. No appointment, referral or diagnosis needed; you can walk in. They're open extended hours. They aren't a crisis service.
  • Medicare Mental Health phone line: 1800 595 212, 8:30am to 5pm weekdays (excluding public holidays; the government page doesn't say which time zone). A trained professional can help you find free or low-cost local services.
  • Medicare Mental Health Check In: free online tools for people 16 and over, for mild mental health challenges, with telehealth support if needed.
  • Bulk-billing professionals: you pay nothing. Healthdirect's service finder can show which ones bulk bill.
  • Public mental health services: usually no cost, usually a wait. See our WA services list.
  • Centrelink social workers: free short-term counselling and referrals. Ask for one through the Centrelink employment services line or at a service centre.

For carers

A carer or family member can attend up to 2 sessions a year as part of the person's plan, without the person there, if the person agrees and their health professional recommends it. These count towards the person's 10 sessions. Centrelink also has Carer Payment and Carer Supplement for people who give constant care.

Source: Services Australia: Mental health care and Medicare · Last checked 30 Oct 2026

Questions to ask before you book

  • "What's your fee, how much will Medicare pay back, and what's my gap?"
  • "Do you bulk bill anyone, for example concession card holders?"
  • "Do you need a referral from my usual GP before the first session?"
  • "Can I pay just the gap on the day, or do I pay the full fee and claim later?"
